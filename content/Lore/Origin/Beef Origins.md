## Chapter 1

In the taverns of the Central Plains, where storytellers traded truth for legend and legend for coin, there was one name that never failed to draw laughter before awe.

"Beef."

No one remembers her real name. She made sure of that. No one remembered who had first called her that. Some claimed it was because she could devour an entire roasted ox before sunset. Others swore they had seen her drain ten jars of strong wine without so much as a stagger. The innkeepers cared little for the truth. Whenever the towering woman crossed their threshold with her great mo blade strapped across her back and spear in hand, they simply sighed, fetched every cut of beef they had, and began counting their losses.

She accepted the nickname with a shrug.

A name was lighter than her blade.

She held the line. When the enemies charged, they found themselves crashing against an immovable mountain of flesh, steel, and unwavering resolve. While others pursued elegant sword arts or nimble footwork, Beef planted her feet, raised her enormous horse cleaving blade, and answered every attack head on until her foes exhausted themselves against her or were in pieces by her feet.

She belonged to the Raging Tide, a fellowship whose members wandered the jianghu like waves upon the sea—sometimes calm, sometimes devastating. Among them, she was less the wave than the reef that shattered fleets.

Yet beneath her carefree appetite lay an old scar.

Years ago, before the empire settled into peace, her family had been swept away in the chaos that accompanied Zhao Kuangyin's rise to power. Whether by the emperor's command or by the ambitions of those who fought beneath his banner no longer mattered to history. The dead remained dead.

Many expected her to seek vengeance.

She never denied it.

Nor did she pursue it.

"The realm has bled enough," she would say before draining another bowl of wine. "If the Son of Heaven keeps the people fed and the roads safe, I'll let the ghosts sleep."

Then, after a long silence, she would add with a smile that never reached her eyes:

"But if he ever becomes another tyrant... I'll sharpen my blade."

So she wandered.

She fought bandits instead of soldiers. She guarded caravans instead of plotting rebellion. She filled her stomach with mountains of beef, emptied taverns of their strongest spirits, and carried the weight of revenge as patiently as she carried the colossal blade upon her back.

In the jianghu, some heroes sought glory.

Some sought justice.

Beef sought another serving.

Everything else could wait.
