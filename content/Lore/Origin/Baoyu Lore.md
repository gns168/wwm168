## Origin

Baoyu is the eldest son of a rich silk merchant. From a young age he was obsessed with stories of the jianghu and wished to become a xia, to the chagrin of his father. Still, the old man indulged him and hired a master for him, hoping that learning martial arts would teach his son some discipline.
A move the merchant would come to bitterly regret when Baoyu reached adulthood. The young lad refused point blank to inherit the family business, arguing that his younger brother would be much more suited for it since he was actually already quite involved in it and had an appetite for the merchant life. His father wouldn’t hear any of it, he was the eldest, so he was the one intended to inherit, whether he liked it or not.
So on a whim, Baoyu ran from home, never to return. He had heard tales of a legendary xia, gnsyiliuba, and had in his mind to become their disciple.
One day, while staying in a small village in Qinghe on his way to find his future master, he accepted to help the villagers who had been harassed for weeks by a group of bandits from Tiger Fort. Being so far from Kaifeng, their pleas for help had been thus far ignored. Sincerely wanting to do some good in this world and live the honorable life of a xia, Baoyu set course to the bandits’ lair.
He was way over his head however, having underestimated the number of bandits. At the brink of death, the gods must have pitied him, for he was saved by a divine being with nine tails. Profusely bleeding and exhausted, he passed out before seeing his savior’s face.
He woke up the next day all patched up in a little cottage in the mountains. While he recalled the events of the day before, he thought he had hallucinated. Surely, nine tail foxes were not real ? Lo and behold, said fox entered the cottage, medicine in hand. Baoyu thought he must have been bashed on his head one too many times. The fox laughed at his reaction and introduced herself as gnsyiliuba. Baoyu could barely believe his luck. Ignoring his wounds, he threw himself on his knees, begging the legendary xia to take him as a disciple. gnsyiliuba called him an idiot and told him she’d think about it once he was healed.
She nursed him back to health for weeks and then challenged him to a duel. She announced that if he managed to land three moves on her, she would
accept him as a disciple. On the brink of his dream coming true, Baoyu gave
his all but only landed two moves. Still, gnsyiliuba was touched by his brave heart
and his determination. He had a good martial fondation and with a bit of
polish, he had the potential to become a great xia. So she nonetheless took
him as a disciple.
Baoyu turned out to be a good student, diligent in his training. Though, in
gnsyiliuba’s mind, he was a peculiar young man. He was strangely coquettish,
more so than some young women she knew, always obsessed with wearing
the finest garments. He would wake up extra early to have the time to take
care of his, admittedly, beautiful hair before training. Still, he was a good
fighter and kind of heart, so his peculiarities didn’t matter much to her.
He was however a bit possessive of his master and did not approve of the
arrival of a mysterious woman, Heka. While he didn’t treat her coldly, he didn’t
make any effort to befriend her. Not that it would discourage the young
woman, who was chaos incarnate, from pursuing him.
